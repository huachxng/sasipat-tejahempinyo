In a relationship, is it better to have a partner who is beautiful but **narcissistic**, or who is **amiable** but ugly? Even though humans are easily attracted to aesthetically pleasing writings, the intended messages' significance has diminished. In many pieces of literature, from the past to the present, the question sparked: what truly defines "excellent work?" Does the beauty of a letter come from the message inside or the decoration from the outside? To attract readers, the exterior look is far more important, but to make them love, the interior is the essence. As both a writer and reader, I have always been fascinated by whether to focus on the outer or inner beauty of my story. In this essay, I will be exploring different types of literature from the poem in the past and the short fiction story in the present day to identify the style of writing from time to time. The answer to the question is whether its purpose was to please the audience, then to send out the message, or vice versa. For the first part, I will be discussing the example from Sonnet 130 written by William Shakespeare; the main focus being the use of metaphor to compare certain features of humans to certain characteristics of non-human objects or beings. Then followed closely by the poem by Oscar Wilde called "Apologia," which Wilde had successfully used sensory description to depict the picture and to hint at the immoral conduct that went against the norms of society at times. With these two examples in the part can see that the culture and people at times might have been more pleased by using the *la forme*. In contrast, at the present day, it was the opposite, whereas the example from the short story mentioned in the third part that supported my claim about *le fond* is far more important than *la forme.* In that part, also, I will also be stating the consulted article about the sexuality and gender inequality mentioned in Asian American Literature. Lastly, I will prove my claim that in the present-day world, emphasizing the use of *le fond* is far more effective and better than *la forme.* To underline the importance of the message intended to say, and not to change the meaning to please the audience. Along with the continuity and change over time, how have literature pieces changed the trend from the past up to the present?

The literary work of love, Sonnet 130, has become an iconic piece of rom-com poetry by using metaphors to illustrate certain features that highlight the extent of beauty. Although the initial tone seems to be **polemical**, as he compares his lover's features to aesthetically displeasing objects to adhere to the structure of a Shakespearean sonnet, the true message is that despite her appearance, he still loves her. This work contradicts its own formatting because, while Shakespeare aimed to achieve a specific aesthetic structure, the intended meaning shifts. The comparison between an object and a woman's features reflects certain societal expectations as mentioned by Shakespeare, such that: "If snow be white, why then her breasts are dun;" (Shakespeare, line 3). Women's breasts are expected to be white, but the lovers' are dun, which means grayish-brown. This can be interpreted to suggest that the mistress did not meet the beauty standard, or perhaps that the structure was more fitted by using the word "dun" to rhyme with "sun." Could it be that the breasts weren't actually colored dun, but rather that the rhyme scheme influenced Shakespeare to change the meaning entirely just to maintain an aesthetically pleasing format for his readers? "If hairs be wires, black wires grow on her head." (line 4). Additionally, another evident use of metaphor in his poem is the comparison of wire to hair, further signifying the negativity of the woman's appearance. Toward the end, Shakespeare adds, "And yet, by heaven, I think my love as rare as any she belied with false compare." (line 14). The juxtaposition of this stanza to the holistic idea throughout supports the claim that in the end, even if the beauty of his mistress does not meet societal standards, his love is quite unique and hard to compare. Thus, it can be concluded that *la forme* is more important than *le fond* during the nineteenth century in order to keep the audience entertained. Meanwhile, Wilde focused more on the poet's diction to distract the audience; can that be considered as purposefully using adjectives to shift the scandalous messages? Next up, this paper will discuss how Oscar Wilde used the poet's diction and use of sensory imagery to attract and repel certain groups of audience and avoid being caught scandalous.

Oscar Wilde has been excellent at effectively using rhetoric to convey his **cryptic** message. However, other groups may only appreciate his style and diction. After analyzing the theme of the poem, it focuses on the **precarious** love of the persona towards another man. The title is derived from the description of "Apologia," meaning to defend its own claim, suggesting that their idea shall not be deemed immoral or against the norm. As prompted in the poem: "Or how the little flower he trod upon," (Wilde, line 25), the use of the pronoun "he" leads the audience to understand that the persona is referring to another man. Additionally, the sensory description "To have walked hand in hand with Love, and seen his purple wings flit once across thy smile" (line 31) emphasizes the beauty of this poem by sharing moments with the persona's beloved via the visual representation, despite society's judgment. This embodied the author's message that something considered 'civilized' in the past should be adjusted to acknowledge that love disregards gender. As demonstrated through literary movements, the message is conveyed subtly using sensory descriptions to represent "love," but societal norms have toned down words to attract an audience. This reflects a time when *la forme* was prioritized over *le fond* to conform to societal acceptance. It supports the trend of the Victorian period, where literature was only accepted with a certain level of allure and should not violate sexual norms. Shifting into more contemporary literature work, "The Kitchen God" has shown a different perspective and approach towards conveying the message to the audience to more directly emphasizing the message that can be related throughout the globe via the same gender inequality issue faced by women across all hemispheres.

In the latest example, "The Kitchen God" explicitly introduces direct and informal diction to convey the author's messages, but it may simultaneously be displeasing to a large group of audiences. This serves as an excellent illustration of a work that prioritizes *le fond* over *la forme*, a trend increasingly evident in today's world. The author's message emerges through the interactions between female and male characters. This piece was written by a Japanese author representing Asian culture, and it was published in the American newspaper The New Yorker, highlighting the crossover of American culture. Many criticisms of this piece focus on the direct and informal diction used to illustrate how women are treated in Asian culture, and somewhat, it shared the same trend in Western culture as well. The main character, Izumi, was sexually harassed by a man named Sanobe. Their relationship is complex because Izumi engages in sexual encounters with Mr. Sanobe in exchange for money; it is heavily implied that Mr. Sanobe only uses her for mere entertainment through **profligacy**. Gender inequality in many Asian societies stems from a patriarchal system. In the article "Asian American Women and Racialized Femininities: "Doing" Gender across Cultural Worlds," Karen D. Pyke discusses how women become subordinated; they must maintain their "femininity as they move between mainstream and ethnic arenas, such as family, work, and school (...)" (Pyke, 34). Women are expected to be feminine, or so-called pretty, to fit into a mainstream and ethically acceptable society. Similar to how Izumi is treated by Mr. Sanobe, she must be a beautiful and loving woman to please a man and subordinate herself financially. It can be concluded that, throughout the contemporary world, the idea of being visually pleasing is necessary for having one's voice considered; however, many literary works have started to emerge with calls for prioritizing message over artistic perspective. Due to the more relatable topics and messages being conveyed, it is much easier for many audiences to connect and engage with stories, as demonstrated by The New Yorker translating the story from Japanese to English for American audiences.

Evidently, there has been a significant shift in literary works from the Victorian era to the present day. The most pronounced change in the contemporary world is the movement from prioritizing *le fond* over *la forme*. Today, authors tend to emphasize the importance of *le fond* to attract an audience. This shift occurs because they are confident that they have a group of readers who will appreciate their work, rather than trying to please social standards. In the example used in this paper, "The Kitchen God," the author employs direct and informal diction to convey the story, informing the audience about gender inequality occurring not only in specific parts of the Eastern hemisphere but throughout the globe. This trend was made possible by the impact of globalization, which broadened the potential audience and resulted in greater acceptance of diverse ideas within social networks. In contrast, during the Victorian era, when "Apologia" and "Sonnet 130" were published, *le forme* was much more pleasing to the audience consuming their works; if it had not been, they could easily have faced **turbulences** for publishing anything against social norms. In conclusion, this paper demonstrates the differences between the past and present through the impact of globalization, showing that prioritization of *le fond* is more common in contemporary works, whereas *la forme* was more preferred in the past. Focusing on a better equalized world nowadays, it is important to focus on the use of *la forme* as the message was able to spread rather than putting efforts into trying to search for the audience in risk changing the meaning holistically.
