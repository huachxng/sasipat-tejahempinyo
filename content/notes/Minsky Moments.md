Hyman Minsky's financial instability hypothesis (Minsky, 1992, Levy Economics Institute Working Paper 74) is one of three theoretical anchors in the literature review of [[AI Bubble Research]], alongside Kindleberger's mania-panic-crash stages and Shiller's irrational exuberance. In popular usage a "Minsky moment" is the point at which debt taken on during calm conditions stops being sustainable and forced selling begins.

## How the paper uses it

The paper maps a five-pattern checklist of historical crises onto its pillars. Rapid price inflation detached from fundamentals feeds valuation; a surge in speculative retail participation feeds sentiment; capital misallocation feeds concentration; the "new paradigm" narrative is discussed qualitatively; and the external shock that ends the episode is treated as a trigger, not a scored condition. Leverage does not map cleanly onto any of the five, but it is central to nearly every crisis narrative, so it became the fourth pillar. The outline calls it "the 2008 amplifier".

Two design choices in the [[Bubble Intensity Score]] follow. The sentiment pillar is inverted so that calm conditions raise the score, because bubbles inflate in calm times. And the trigger stays outside the equation; the [[Geopolitical Risk Index]] gives that section a quantitative hook without turning the shock into a pillar.

## What 2008 showed

The 2008 backtest is the paper's clearest Minsky-style episode. The composite never rose above +0.43 in the 2003-2008 run-up. The leverage pillar, on its own, peaked at +2.11 in December 2007, four months after BNP Paribas froze redemptions on three funds and nine months before Lehman. Under the earlier corporate-debt-only proxy it had reached +1.43 in July 2007. Valuation and concentration sat at or below their decade averages the whole time. The crisis was in credit, not in equity prices, and an equity-centric composite averaged the one real signal away.

That is why the paper reports all four pillars rather than the headline number, and why the leverage reading of -0.05 in June 2026 matters as much as the valuation reading of +2.14. The cash-funded-capex view in [[Counterarguments to the AI Bubble Thesis]] is essentially a claim that no Minsky-style build-up shows in listed data; the paper's caveat is that private credit and vendor financing sit outside those series.
