One common misunderstanding of the concept of possession is that possession means individual's complete ownership and earned through efforts. Frankly, I see it differently in the different perspective. Possession doesn't have to be 'individual' or solely done by one person. One thing can be possessed by multiple parties and shared experiences are hard to claim as one's own 'possession.' Despite the value of possession is the default human's nature to feel the need to own or control things they earned, we often ignore the importances of shared possession with loved ones; Baca's claim on value of possession is legit to certain extent, but still closed an eye on something that is, arguably, far more important: shared journey.

Nobel Prizes of many sciences and literatures are one of the greatest achievement for individual to possess. You get to be either the first, the fastest, or the fabulous of that talent. Many strive to earn that prize to make it their own possession of success, but they often forget to look back behind them the shared value with their colleagues. Numerous success and possession cannot possibly be claimed by one's sole research or making, they were, rather, the contribution from the past to present. For instance, how Atomic Models are from times to times change and improve to get the most accurate representation of our atoms. Without JJ Thomson's initiative of the plum pudding model, there may not be the Rutherford's Nuclear model. Unexpectedly, they were, at times, close friends and colleagues who have the determination to create the more accurate atomic models. From the surface, or what we had learned in many western curricula, they seemed to be individuals who fiercely compete to discover the better models just like how in many curricula painted them to only see the value of their individual success, though in reality, it is much different. They sure valued their possession and their proud success, but it is also that they cherished the colleagueship along the way within the scientific fields and shared interest.

As Baca's claim states such that people "would … rather talk about" their new achievement from their own hands like getting "a new car" over to tell the "story … between grandfather and me." Or even how we'd rather spend time talking about "epics in our own lives" than just running the "computer and play video games and enact some cataclysmic epic." Interestingly enough, this has been the trend of what modern day is. We seek the pleasure and social interaction through social media, less of face to face drinking coffee or tea in the nearby cafe. The urge to present that valuable shared journey of one's relationship with the close friends or partner has greatly been reduced over the few years of what to called exponential technology growth. We tend to focus more into the ongoing possession to keep up with mainstream. This is applied thoroughly to many recent generations like the Gen Z's or the Alphas. Increasingly, we tend to seek possession from buying what's trendy to show in our story in Instagram, and inversely proportional to a moment shared in the park with your best friend. From this observation that doesn't only happen in my friends' circle, but rather throughout the entire internet, especially in the platform like Instagram, Facebook, or even WhatsApp. Baca is right, but not all, people value possessions, but the possessions in question has been shifted from the shared to individualistic.

This is how our society has changed. I adore the past where minimalized computer is not enough to fit in our pocket or easily carried around like a baggage; we see people picnicking by double, if not more, in the park–having real conversation and not via phone. Not only for American, but the value of possessions have been more prominent in one's mind, undeniably, because of social expectation. We shifted from shared possession with meaningful interaction into individualistic success. I must admit, it is quite satisfactory to claim something is done solely from your blood and sweat, but tell me, do you truly value that or let the society determines whether that is valuable or not?
