Dear Ms. Allen,

I am currently a student in grade 11 at BASIS International School Bangkok and currently enrolling in the AP Language and Composition class. I have received your concern regarding the advanced level of the media (texts) used in AP Language and Composition that is not sufficient for student's college preparation and you find it inappropriate due to racial stereotypes used in the book thus becoming the reason why the book is being temporarily banned at the moment. I will confess that initially, I also found that this book touches on the offensive visual representation of stereotypes of Asian ethnicity represented by the character Chin-kee. I was worried and questioned if this book is school appropriate or not, but once I have completed the book–it totally introduces the reader to a different standpoint. This book demands a high attention and skill of analysis for each panel with hidden details that contribute to the true meaning intended by the author–that very much meets the requirements of AP Language and Composition CED. I am writing this email to you to offer some of the reasons why this book should not be banned whether due to the literary level or the inappropriateness that may have been misunderstood.

In the book *American Born Chinese*, the story portrays three different perspectives: Jin, the Monkey King, and Danny with his cousin Chin-kee. All three protagonists all share the same identity-trauma and suffered greatly from stereotypes that eventually lead to discrimination and verbal harassment. In one scene where Jin and his Asian-descendant friends are chatting, they are all harassed by racially offensive words by White students. Furthermore, another controversial character like Chin-kee is the incarnated symbol of the Chinese stereotypes many Westerners tend to think of. This idea directly corresponds with my presentation about the Dr. Fu Manchu series where many of the visual characteristics of Chin-kee are shared with Dr. Fu Manchu with long braided hair, slanted eyes, and wearing ancient Chinese robes. Another notable but subtle indication of Asian stereotype is presented in the chapter of his visit to Danny–he participates in the class by answering all advanced academic questions correctly. Again, it is a common representation of how Asians are inherently smart. All of those scenes are a great representation of what most Asian-American or Asian descendant students in Western schools have to go through studying abroad. Initially, many will find it offensive of portraying offensive stereotypes against Asian descendants, but this is the intended message the author, Gene Yang, wants to address to his audience. The readers may have easily missed this without careful analysis of each panel not only through texts, but also visual images being portrayed which is also very crucial when approaching advanced courses like AP Language and Composition.

I personally agree that much advanced literature does require a complex analysis and sympathetic trait in order to truly understand the author's intention of the book, which is why I think that *American Born Chinese* is a book worthy of being taught in any high school English class at BASIS, and not only just Mr. Feldman's. Even though Dr. Fredric Wertham, Child Psychologist, concluded that comic books lead to juvenile delinquency in many of the students, there are many benefits to comics that stand out compared to other mediums used by many high school English classes. For instance, comic panels are permanent and physical for students to learn through visuals. From my first-hand middle school experience, I learned the majority of my English reading proficiency through reading comic books. I managed to read in a better flow by setting my pace at how fast I would like to read each dialogue without having to be set by the teachers. Yang also emphasises this in his TED Talk from his experience of teaching high school students through medium like comic panels. It is proven that he gained a lot of positive feedback from his students.

In conclusion, I think it is very important for students of AP Language and Composition to be exposed to various mediums of literature to enhance their learning capability and analysis of visual images. Even though there may be offensive contents, there are always intended messages authors wish to convey. This not only encourages students to spend time carefully analyzing the texts, but also creates sympathetic traits needed for them to understand authors' intentions on each piece of literature. As it may seem that *ABC* can have various methods–that some can be highly offensive–of how authors decided to approach stereotypes as to address his personal experience as Asian descendant students in Western school. This book should not be banned, and instead encouraged to be used as a medium of study where students can also have control of their own time to understand the book and its intended message.
