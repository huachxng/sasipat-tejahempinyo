The [[AI Bubble Research]] paper handles objections in steelman, response, concession order. The five that matter:

## 1. You cannot call a bubble in real time (Fama, 2014)

Strongest form: "bubble" implies prices must fall; crashes are visible only in hindsight; so any real-time label is unfalsifiable. Response: the [[Bubble Intensity Score]] never predicts a crash; it measures resemblance to past pre-crash configurations. Concession: a high reading that never ends in a crash is a live possibility.

## 2. High prices can be rational in a technological revolution (Pástor and Veronesi, 2006, 2009)

Strongest form: under genuine uncertainty about a transformative technology, high valuations and concentration are what rational pricing looks like, so V and C would flash in a rational boom too. Response: the index flags statistical abnormality, not irrationality; the two are observationally equivalent in real time. Concession: the word "bubble" in the title is an interpretation, not a measurement.

## 3. The valuation input is biased (Siegel, 2016)

Strongest form: accounting changes and buybacks make modern CAPE structurally higher than its long history. Response: the z-score uses a trailing ten-year window, not the full century, which nets out most of the level bias. Concession: buyback effects sit inside recent windows.

## 4. Three episodes, chosen after the fact

Strongest form: with N = 3 anything fits, and any indicator watched publicly loses its signal. Response: the success criteria were written before the backtest ran, and the composite honestly missed 2008 rather than being re-tuned; see [[Minsky Moments]]. Concession: N = 3 is N = 3.

## 5. AI is cash-funded, not debt-funded (Goldman Sachs, January 2026)

Strongest form: hyperscalers fund capex from operating cash flow, and AI capex is roughly 0.8% of GDP against 1.5% or more in past infrastructure booms, so there is no crisis mechanism. Response: this is what the leverage pillar says, with one caveat: official corporate-debt series miss private credit, vendor financing and circular financing of AI capex, so L could understate fragility. Concession: if the claim holds, any "crisis" is a valuation event, not a systemic one.

The literature review adds two critiques of composite indices: weights reflect past crises, so novel channels get zero weight; and financial conditions indexes do not reliably beat a plain stock index (Hatzius et al., 2010). The pillar decomposition and the [[Geopolitical Risk Index]] upgrade are partial answers.
