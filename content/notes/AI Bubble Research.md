My independent economics paper. Working title: "AI Bubble: The Mere Future Crisis". The SSRN title will most likely be "The Bubble Intensity Score: A Simple Composite Index for Situating the 2025-2026 AI Market Among Historical Bubbles".

## The question

Can the structural state of a financial bubble be captured in a single, simple composite measure, and where does the AI market sit on it relative to historical bubbles?

The answer the data gives so far: the 2025-2026 market carries the Dot-com signature on valuation and concentration, but the leverage pillar that defined 2007 sits at its decade average. Expensive like 1999, not fragile like 2007. That points to correction risk rather than systemic collapse. The index itself is described in [[Bubble Intensity Score]].

## Why it exists

Experts have GSADF tests, VAR models and CAPE. Ordinary investors have headlines. The paper's "so what" is financial literacy: a number a non-specialist can compute from free public data and read without panicking or chasing hype. The [[Geopolitical Risk Index]] entered the paper partly for that reason. It is a Federal Reserve economists' index built by counting newspaper articles, which makes the news-literacy argument concrete.

## Structure

Seven chapters: introduction; literature review (historical narratives, bubble theory including [[Minsky Moments]], and quantitative detection); methodology; data; results; discussion; conclusion. Appendices cover data sources, robustness tables and the replication pipeline. A dedicated section handles the [[Counterarguments to the AI Bubble Thesis]].
